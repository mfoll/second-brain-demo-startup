# Write Before You Meet

## Stance

For a mostly remote team, a decision meeting should start from a written proposal that everyone has read. The meeting is for disagreement, not for discovering what the proposal is.

## Scope

Decisions and design reviews. Not one-to-ones, retros or social time.

## Why I Think This

- The code review decision ([[engineering-meeting-2026-04-08]]) went well because the options were written two days before; Hamish's dissent was sharp because he had read them.
- The architecture review of 2025-11-18 had a written proposal; the one meeting in 2025 without one ran 90 minutes and decided nothing.
- People who think better in writing, or in their second language, get a fair chance.

## What Would Change My Mind

Decisions slowing down because nobody writes the proposal. That happens when the owner is unclear, which is a delegation problem, not a writing one.

## Implications

No agenda document, no decision. I hold myself to it least well of anyone; see [[are-written-updates-hiding-problems]].
