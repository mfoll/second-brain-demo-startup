# Determinants Of Information Security Policy Compliance: Integrating Task-Technology Fit And Threat Avoidance Appraisals In The Digital Workplace

Alassaf M, Alkhalifah A. Frontiers in Psychology, 2026.

## Summary

A survey of 288 employees across public and private sectors, modeled with PLS-SEM, combining task-technology fit, technology threat avoidance theory and the theory of planned behavior. Fit between tasks and security technology, together with self-efficacy, social influence and perceived policy effectiveness, predicted intention to comply with security policy and then behavior. Perceived costs of complying reduced intention; perceived threat had no significant effect.

## Why It Matters

It gives a vocabulary for something I argue in [[compliance-should-be-a-byproduct-of-engineering-practice]]: people follow security rules that fit how they already work and that do not cost them much. The quarterly access review failed in the Type I partly because it fit nobody's tools. The finding that perceived threat did not matter is a caution for the 2027 training: scary examples are not the lever.

## Caveats

Self-reported intention and behavior; employees in general workplaces, not software engineers. Association, not causation.
