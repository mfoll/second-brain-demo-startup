# Planning fallacy

Wikipedia article, last edited 2026-09-11.

## Summary

Describes the planning fallacy, a cognitive bias in which people underestimate how long their own future tasks will take, even when they know that similar tasks in the past ran over. Outside observers tend to err in the other direction.

## Why It Matters

Sigrid Aaltonen sent me this article after I told her about the version 3 slip. It fits part of the story: I planned the tenant migration pilot's end date as if this time it would end on schedule, against our own history of platform work running long. It does not fit the feature estimates, which were roughly right. Discussed in [[lessons-from-the-version-3-slip]] and behind the stance in [[dates-are-promises-only-when-contractual]].

## Caveats

General psychology, not software-specific evidence. The remedy we took (capacity reservations, appetites) is ours, not the article's.
