# Dates Are Promises Only When Contractual

## Stance

A date we give a customer is a promise. We should make promises only when we will move capacity to keep them, which in practice means contractual dates. Everything else is a target, and we should say so.

## Scope

Customer-facing dates and board-level commitments. Internal targets are fine and useful; they just should not leak.

## Why I think this

- In 2026 we kept exactly one date: Meridale's SSO, which was contractual. We treated it as fixed and split it out of 3.0 to protect it. We missed the 3.0 date by twelve weeks.
- The cost of the miss was not engineering; it was a month of customer success calls and a credibility dent with accounts that had planned training.
- Our estimates are not the problem. Capacity we did not control was. The [[planning-fallacy]] describes the general pattern of underestimating the time for one's own plans; in our case the base rate we ignored was how often platform work runs over.

## What would change my mind

A year in which the release train hits every quarterly target. Then sharing dates earlier would be cheap.
