# Who Should Own Security When We Reach Twenty People?

Today the security program is me (engineering controls, questionnaires, SOC 2 coordination) and [[colette-fairbanks]] two days a week (people and vendor controls). With the extension, Quaylane may reach about 20 people by the end of 2027. Who should own security then?

## Why It Matters

- SOC 2 work took about 22 of my hours during the two weeks of Type I fieldwork and takes 4 or 5 hours a month in the Type II window, on top of leading the team.
- Questionnaires are growing: 9 in 2026 against 3 in 2025.
- Both Type I exceptions were in controls nobody had time to own properly ([[lessons-from-the-soc2-type-1-audit]]).

## Options So Far

1. Keep it with me, delegate controls to engineers by area. Cheapest; keeps security close to engineering decisions.
2. Give a platform engineer, if we hire one first, a defined share of security ([[should-we-hire-a-platform-engineer-or-keep-everyone-on-call]]). [[kwabena-asare]] likes this; it changes the job description.
3. A part-time or fractional security lead from 2027-H2. Most expertise, least context.

## Leaning

Option 1 through the Type II report, then decide with the 2027 hiring result in hand. Not a reason by itself to choose the platform engineer.
