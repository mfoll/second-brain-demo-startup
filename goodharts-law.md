# Goodhart's law

Wikipedia article, last edited 2026-10-09.

## Summary

The idea that a number stops telling you much once people are rewarded for moving it. Named after the economist Charles Goodhart, who expressed the core idea in a 1975 article on monetary policy, observing that statistical regularities tend to collapse once they are used for control.

## Why It Matters

The reason I refuse per-engineer metrics and keep the [[dora-four-keys]] at team level. Also the main risk in [[which-engineering-metrics-should-we-show-the-board]]: a number the board sees each quarter becomes a target whether anyone says so or not. Pages per week is the clearest case; it would be easy to "improve" it by demoting alerts that should page.

## Caveats

An adage, not a finding. Useful as a check on a metric proposal, not as a reason to measure nothing.
