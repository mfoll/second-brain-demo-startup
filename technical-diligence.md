# Technical Diligence

The part of an investor's [[due-diligence]] that tests whether the product and the engineering organization can carry the company's plan. At seed stage it is usually one person from the fund, a request list and one or two calls.

## What It Covered for Us

From Copperwell's [[copperwell-diligence-request-list]] and the [[technical-diligence-call-2026-08-19]]:

- Architecture and its recent changes, especially reversals.
- Security and compliance evidence: SOC 2 status, pen test, questionnaire answers.
- Reliability history: incidents, postmortems, on-call.
- Team risk: who knows what, hiring plan, [[bus-factor]].
- Unit costs: [[cloud-cost-per-clinic]].

## What I Learned

- Investors read decisions and reversals as signals about judgment. A reversal explained with numbers and a clear trigger reads well; a reversal discovered in the data room reads badly.
- They test claims against evidence. Every claim in the data room should point to something: a report, a dashboard, a postmortem.
- The finding that matters is usually something the team already knows. Ours was the bus factor.
