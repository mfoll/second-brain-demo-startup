# Code review

Wikipedia article, last edited 2026-10-05.

## Summary

Code review is a quality assurance activity in which one or more people examine a program's source code, after implementation or during development. The people checking are reviewers, and at least one of them must not be the author. The article covers review types, from formal inspection to lightweight tool-based review, and their reported effects.

## Why It Matters

The baseline definition behind our [[code-review-policy]]: the "not the author" condition is the one that GitHub enforces and the auditor checks. Under a broad reading, pair programming with a navigator is a form of review during development, which is the core of Hamish's argument ([[should-pair-programmed-commits-count-as-reviewed]]). Our rule takes the narrower, tool-recorded form because that is what produces evidence.
